Argentina
Union divisions to contain labour disruption risk into 2027

A march by labour unions on 22 July in Buenos Aires over employment, pension and salary demands drew a lower-than-expected turnout.

  • Disagreements between labour unions and unions’ lack of confidence in the level of support for a general strike will continue to limit unions’ mobilisation capacity.
  • Concerns over falling salaries, mounting corruption cases and persistent unemployment will sustain labour and broader protest activity ahead of the 2027 general election, but mobilisation will likely remain localised.
  • Businesses are likely to face operational disruption, including access constraints and traffic delays, in central Buenos Aires and other urban centres.

Union divisions

Argentina’s unions remain influential in wage negotiations, especially in the transport, financial services and agro-industrial sectors. The most influential labour organisations formally backed pensioners on 22 July on their weekly march to the Congress in Buenos Aires. However, participation was uneven, with more confrontational unions driving the mobilisation, while several of the CGT’s most powerful unions only had a limited presence.

Political pressure

The mobilisation took place amid growing dissatisfaction with Milei’s administration. A survey conducted on 3-8 July showed government approval at 34% and disapproval at 62%, with the main public concerns being unemployment (38%), corruption (38%) and low salaries (35%).

Outlook

The gap between improving macroeconomic indicators and persistent household concerns over employment, wages and purchasing power will remain a source of political vulnerability for Milei ahead of the 2027 election.

Protests will likely remain localised, especially in central Buenos Aires, unless unions align behind a general strike and secure broader worker participation, which is still unlikely.

Sources: Clarín, University of San Andrés, AtlasIntel/Bloomberg, Moody’s Ratings, Control Risks
