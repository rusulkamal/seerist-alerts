Seerist · Alert emails · July 2026 Concept design

Handoff notes

What the redesign changes, what it needs from the data, and what is still open. Written for whoever builds it. Nothing here is scheduled; treat it as the design brief rather than a ticket.

Read this first

Three things that change how you should read everything below.

This covers 2 of your 12 live templates

The production set splits into two generations with incompatible data shapes. The content.* pair (single and batch) is what this redesign replaces. The other ten are flat and snake_case (num_nearby_assets, common_src) and share no field names with it. "The alerts redesign" sounds broader than what exists.

Grouping, capping and banding all happen server-side

The template stays substitution and loops. It cannot group by country, chunk a list into pairs, band a severity score, or compare two dates. Anything of that kind is listed in the data contract below.

The previews are real rendered output

Every example on this site comes from running the actual template through a Handlebars engine with SendGrid's helper set, using real content from the inbox. Asset names are replaced with placeholders because the site is public; everything else is real.

Behaviours worth knowing

Short, and mostly invisible until one of them is missed.

  • Everything is capped server-side. Six locations, three sources, six assets, fifty items. The template prints whatever remainder it is handed as +N more; it cannot count or truncate.
  • Tables, not CSS layout. Two-column anything is table cells. The live template styles assets with column-count: 2 and floats the header, and Outlook honours neither, so twenty-five assets arrive as one twenty-five-line column.
  • Every jump anchor needs clicktracking="off". Without it SendGrid rewrites the href and the jump list silently stops working.
  • Small text now passes contrast. Metadata moves to #6B7280 at 4.83:1 and links to #00702B at 6.26:1. The live values measure 3.59:1 and 2.54:1, both under the 4.5:1 that WCAG AA wants at this size.
  • The country prints once. Today Countries: Saudi Arabia renders directly beneath a Saudi Arabia heading, with Cities: as a third line. Now it is one row.
  • The status row is gone. It printed Status: Breaking under a BREAKING chip. The spec does document a Status property, so this is a deliberate deviation, not an omission.

What the backend needs to supply

Everything else uses fields the builder already emits. These are the exceptions, and the ones marked "confirm" are genuine questions rather than instructions.

One technical risk

Most metadata fields are optional, so each one has to work out whether it needs a middot in front of it or is the first thing on the line. The template answers that with nested or subexpressions, up to thirteen deep on the last field.

or appears nowhere in your twelve production templates, and nothing in that codebase nests subexpressions deeper than one level. It renders correctly in a standard Handlebars engine, but SendGrid runs its own and this is well past anything your setup has been proven to do. If it fails you would see middots in the wrong places, or that line disappearing.

The fix is small and it is a backend job: join the present metadata fields into one ready-made content.metaLine string and print that. The template then never asks the question.

Still open

None of these block starting on the two templates.

Sub-grouping inside long country sections

A Must-have. With 31 US items the section is one unbroken run. Grouping should be by content type, not category. Needs items pre-grouped by type within each country.

Cross-delivery-path consistency

The Must-have that an alert type looks identical whether it arrives via Alerts or via Search is not addressed. Realistically it depends on whether Generation B migrates onto this shape.

Unsubscribe link

A Should-have, and absent from all twelve production templates too, so it is new build either way. Spec calls for it to require login and route to the alerts table with the Edit modal open.

Risk lists parents alongside their own children

Real values from the inbox: Security, Operational, Maritime, Maritime: War and Operational, Security, Political, Operational: Labour, Security: Civil Unrest, Political: Stability. Six values carrying three values of information. Seen on three separate alerts, so it looks systematic. Better fixed upstream than capped in the template.

Severity barely exists in the data

Across 45 real items, severity is present on 6 and categories on 16. The severity chip is good design serving very little data on today's feed. Worth confirming whether that reflects the pipeline or just these samples.

Two production bugs found while reading the templates

Ten of the twelve load the logo over plain http, which corporate gateways block and Gmail proxies. And in a real received alert the logo slot is empty, no <img> at all. Separately, {{#if country}} wraps the whole heading block in Hotspot and Risk Rating, so a city-only hotspot or a water-only maritime rating renders no heading whatsoever.

Curation is out of scope, and it shows

The Inbox audit's sharpest finding was a fatal shooting rendered with the same weight as a tweet about a commemorative coin. Severity scoring was deferred, so that survives. This redesign makes the email legible, not curated.
